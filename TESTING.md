# Private beta test

## Who to invite

Start with 5–10 designers who have presented work to founders, executives, product leaders, or clients. Ask each person to use a real frame they already understand—not a polished demo supplied by us.

## What to say

> This is an early private test of a Figma plugin for practicing difficult design conversations. Select one frame, explain what it is trying to accomplish, and respond naturally to the boss. The frame is analyzed once and is not saved by our backend. The conversation disappears when you close the plugin. Please do not use work your employer prohibits sending to third-party AI services.

## What not to explain first

Do not explain EgoMeter, Bullshit Detector, Chaos, or the intended strategy. We want to learn whether the conversation itself makes sense without a product tour.

Tell testers that the beta records anonymous daily usage totals, never individual activity. These counters exclude frames, frame names, design intent, dialogue, reports, access codes, IP addresses, and persistent identifiers.

## Feedback questions

1. At any point, did the boss feel uncomfortably recognizable? What did it say?
2. Did the boss ever make a legitimately useful point?
3. Did you feel pressure to defend, concede, clarify, or propose a test?
4. When did the boss feel too smart, too stupid, too agreeable, or too repetitive?
5. Did you trust the privacy explanation enough to use a real frame? Why or why not?
6. Would you voluntarily run another design through it?
7. What happened technically, if anything, that interrupted the experience?

## Signal to watch

The primary signal is not whether testers say the idea is funny. It is whether they voluntarily bring the boss back for a second meeting.

For now, ask testers to paste particularly good or bad dialogue into their feedback manually. The product does not archive sessions.
